A democratized access policy—removing departmental and financial barriers to cluster accounts-enabled broad adoption across student populations ranging from wet-lab biologists to working business professionals. Common pedagogical challenges included HPC onboarding, software dependency management, and large-dataset transfer; shared solutions included priority course scheduling, scaffolded desktop-to-cluster exercises, and modular training materials developed by Clemson's Research Computing and Data Engagement (RCDE) team. Discipline-specific computational profiles varied from large-dataset I/O in bioinformatics and healthcare to GPU-intensive deep learning in automotive AI and intensive floating-point computation in chemistry and parallel architectures. Three transferable principles emerge: democratized access, guaranteed priority scheduling for courses, and domain-contextualized scaffolded exercises. Students completing these courses gain Linux proficiency, job scheduling, parallel programming, and largescale data analysis skills directly applicable to research and industry careers in an economy increasingly dependent on dataintensive computation." keywords: - "research computing" - "workforce development" - "interdisciplinary education" subjects: - "Information Technology" - "Data Science" - "Engineering" audiences: - "Educator" education-levels: - "Graduate/Professional" ---
